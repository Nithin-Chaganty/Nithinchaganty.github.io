I didn't even know where Guatemala was on a map.

I saw a video on social media of someone hiking an active volcano and I booked the flight. I assumed it would be tropical because the only Latin American place I knew was Puerto Rico.

I got to Antigua and checked into my hostel. I love hostels. You meet people from all over the world, different countries, different backgrounds, and sometimes you find out they live twenty minutes from you back home. That night I met people from everywhere and almost all of them told my friend and I the same thing without us asking. Go to sleep early. The hike is tough.

We hung out until 2am.

Only in Guatemala for so long.

We got up at 6am. Started the hike around 10. Volcán Acatenango. 13,000 feet of elevation in five hours. How bad could it be.

Straight elevation the whole way, carrying 40 to 50 pounds on my back filled with food, water, and layers for the cold. Five hours later we reached base camp and I was done. Ready to eat and sleep. I had zero intention of hiking Fuego, the second volcano. Everyone said it was much harder than Acatenango.

I was already falling asleep when my friend and a few others barged into my room and begged me to come. The group was leaving for Fuego at 8pm.

Someone said you'll never be here again.

I put on my gear.

To get to Fuego you descend down the other side of Acatenango, climb up Fuego, then reverse the whole thing to get back to base camp. We reached the viewing point at 11pm. Being that close to an active volcano is something I can't properly put into words. The ground shook every twenty minutes when it erupted. You feel it in your chest. You feel it in your feet. Standing there watching the lava I knew in the back of my mind we still had to make the whole journey back.

After the last eruption we took it in one more time, ash and all, and started moving.

Getting down was easy. My knees are steel. I don't understand why people say down is harder than up.

Going back up to base camp was the hardest thing I've ever done. Morale was low. Nobody was talking. Everyone was just moving. We got back at 2am.

What felt almost impossible in the moment turned into one of the most memorable experiences of my life.

Bite off more than you can chew, then figure out how to chew it later.

Right now Volcán Fuego is unpredictable. Hikes have been stopped and a state of emergency has been declared. I hope it settles. But if it ever opens again, go. Don't sleep early. Bring too much water. Say yes when someone barges into your room at 8pm.

You'll never be here again.
